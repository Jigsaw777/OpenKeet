# Where OpenKeet should launch

Sources checked 7–8 November 2026, India time. Priorities reflect fit, the likelihood of useful feedback, and the work one maintainer can support. They are planning judgments. Wait for the approved public release and test the links while signed out.

## First wave

| Priority and channel | Audience and value | Best post and stage | Rules, links, and effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. GitHub** | The destination for installs, issues, and contributors. Keep it the source of truth. | On day one, pair a release with a short setup discussion. Pin a verified first-run guide and starter issues. | Use the canonical release and issue links. Keep support in Q&A and reproducible defects in Issues. Medium setup, low weekly upkeep. [GitHub Discussions](https://docs.github.com/en/discussions/quickstart) |
| **2. LinkedIn** | Candidates, engineers, and professional contacts. Good for feedback on the preparation flow. | One founder post with a real screenshot or short captioned recording after release. Follow with a technical lesson when there is new evidence. | A direct release link is useful. Use text or a native media post as the composer allows. Share real experience and follow professional policies. Low effort. [Posting guide](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a518996) · [Community policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies) |
| **3. Reddit** | A focused venue can produce detailed setup feedback or contributors. | Start with one eligible community. Use a direct source link, one real demo, and one concrete question. | Apply each community's policy, flair, thread, and authorship requirements. Avoid identical cross-posts. Medium effort because replies matter. [Reddit matrix](reddit-community-matrix.md) · [Reddit spam policy](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam) |
| **4. YouTube** | Candidates who need to see setup and developers who want reproducible evidence. | Record a short intro and a searchable walkthrough after the final build. Put the technical demo out when people ask how it works. | Use a direct release link in the long-form description where clickable links are enabled. Shorts description and comment URLs are non-clickable. Use captions and synthetic data. Medium to high effort. [Link formats](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639) · [External link policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9054257) |

## Second wave

| Priority and channel | Audience and value | Best post and stage | Rules, links, and effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **5. DEV Community** | Desktop, Rust, and AI developers. Good for reproducibility and contributors. | Publish the technical article after the release with code and benchmark links. | Review every claim and disclose AI-assisted drafting in the article. Keep the engineering explanation useful on its own. Medium effort. [DEV AI-assisted article guidelines](https://dev.to/guidelines-for-ai-assisted-articles-on-dev/) |
| **6. Hacker News** | Technical readers who can try and critique the actual project. | Submit a human-authored Show HN once the app is accessible without a gated signup. Stay available to answer technical questions. | Link the actual project or demo that can be tried. HN bars generated submissions and generated or AI-edited comments. The [HN prep](hacker-news.md) is private reference. Medium effort. [Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html) · [HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html) |
| **7. Local AI and desktop communities** | Ollama users, speech developers, and Windows and Tauri builders. Best for integration and hardware feedback. | Share one reproducible Windows setup question or a matched synthetic benchmark after release. | Use only channels that welcome a project link. r/LocalLLaMA requires primarily human-written content and disclosed affiliation. A Discord server can set its own showcase rules. Medium effort. [Ollama project](https://github.com/ollama/ollama) · [Tauri project](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri) · [Reddit matrix](reddit-community-matrix.md) |
| **8. Product Hunt** | People trying new products. Feedback and downloads can help once onboarding works. | Launch after clean first-run trials. Use the working release destination, real gallery images, and a maker comment. | Provide a usable product URL and ask for feedback. Product Hunt prohibits asking for upvotes or offering incentives for them. Higher effort because launch-day replies take time. [Launch guide](https://www.producthunt.com/launch) · [Promotion rules](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/17734384-5-how-to-promote-your-launch-and-what-to-avoid) |
| **9. Indie Hackers** | Solo builders interested in operating costs and scope choices. Useful for product feedback. | After the first setup reports, share what keeping inference local or user-configured made easier and harder. | The site and products directory were visible. Posting and promotional rules could not be verified from the public sources retrieved, so check the current group policy before linking. Medium effort. [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/) |

## Selective follow-ups

| Channel | Fit and use | Link, policy, and effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hashnode | Developer readers. Adapt the DEV article after it has a tested example. | Use a reference to the public source in a useful article. Check editor and attribution settings if republishing. The terms require review of AI suggestions and prohibit spam. Medium effort. [Hashnode terms](https://hashnode.com/terms) |
| Hugging Face | Speech-model maintainers and reproducibility readers. Share a focused model or export issue when one exists. | Use the relevant repository's Discussion to provide a synthetic fixture and version. A GitHub link is context for a real upstream question. Check redistribution before sharing weights or data. Medium effort. [Hub Discussions and PRs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/main/repositories-pull-requests-discussions) |
| Discord | Tauri, Rust, or local AI peers. Good for one implementation question or approved showcase. | Use the server's named channel and ask moderators if its rules are unclear. Share source links when they help reproduce the issue. Never mass-DM. Low to medium effort. [Discord guidelines](https://discord.com/guidelines) · [Tauri project](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri) |
| Slack | Documentation peers can review the first-run guide or contributor process. | Write the Docs publishes specific self-promotion and channel guidance. Use a relevant feedback channel under those rules. Low effort. [Write the Docs Slack](https://www.writethedocs.org/slack/) |
| X | People following a build log or a technical niche. Useful as a lightweight follow-up. | Post a real before-and-after screenshot or one measured lesson with context and the official link. Avoid repetitive link posts, automated replies, and artificial engagement. Low effort. [X authenticity policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity) |
| Open-source and software directories | People searching for Windows alternatives. Try a single AlternativeTo listing after release. | Link the real project site or release and submit accurate licensing, platform, and description for moderation. Avoid categories that imply a hostable server. Low effort. [AlternativeTo FAQ](https://alternativeto.net/faq/) |
| Technical newsletters | Rust and desktop readers. A verified article or measured improvement is a better pitch than a launch announcement. | Use a newsletter's own contribution path. A technical article link is the evidence. No placement or response is assumed. Low effort after the article exists. [This Week in Rust contribution guide](https://this-week-in-rust.org/contribute/) |

For the first week, prioritize the release page, one LinkedIn post, and at most one eligible Reddit post. Use questions from those channels to decide whether a walkthrough or technical article should be next.
